# Brand Muse Bible — v1 (working draft)

**Purpose.** This document defines two recurring brand muses so they stay recognisable and consistent across every campaign, drop, and platform. It is a living reference. Treat it as the single source of truth for who the muses are, how they look, and how to generate or photograph them without drifting.

**How to use it.** Lock the two identities below, then never re-invent them. Every future image starts from a fixed reference of the same face (see *The Consistency System*). The written specs are for briefing photographers, designers, and AI image tools alike.

> One framing note before you read on. These muses are *inspired by* the Parke world, not a copy of it. Parke is the directional reference for tone and styling. The faces, names, and brand world here are your own. See *Risk & Compliance Flags* for why that distinction matters in practice.

> **MAREN note (added during build).** This bible's styling references (vintage denim, mockneck logo sweatshirt, claw clip, iced matcha) come from the Parke world. MAREN's own product is coastal linen and cotton. On MAREN surfaces, keep the muse **faces and DNA** from this document but dress them in MAREN product (linen, breton, gingham, washed cotton) and coastal light. See the storefront `#muses` section for the translated public copy. The two public-facing names are **Cami** (= IVORY) and **Vivi** (= GOLDEN).

## 1. The reference read: what the Parke world actually is

Drawn from Parke's own site and press, not assumption:

- Founded 2022 by Chelsea Parke (Kramer), Miami-based, with an art-history background. The brand began with vintage-inspired denim: structured fits, minimal washes, a worn-in feel. "Intentional but unfussy."
- Positioning is timeless, elevated everyday essentials. Well-made basics built to last years, not seasons.
- The category is elevated casual: subtle logos, modern silhouettes, "casual but considered."
- The channel is TikTok-native and community-led. The brand reads as authentic and personal rather than glossy and corporate. Signature touchpoints include the mockneck logo sweatshirt and the viral "claw clip" hairstyle.

**What this means for the muses.** The world is warm, bright, and American girl-next-door, built on denim, knits, neutrals, and sunlight. It is aspirational but reachable. This is a correction worth stating plainly: it is *not* cold Scandinavian minimalism, which is where our first studio pass leaned. The muses should feel like someone you could know, shot in warm natural-feeling light, wearing real product.

## 2. Shared Muse DNA

Both muses share this. It is what makes them read as one brand:

- **Colouring:** blonde and creamy, as briefed. The split between them is *tone*, not category (warm-golden vs cool-ivory).
- **Build:** slim, natural, healthy. Long lines, relaxed rather than rigid.
- **Face & makeup:** fresh, clean, minimal. "Clean girl" finish: glowing skin, groomed brows, nude or soft tones, nothing heavy.
- **Light & setting:** warm, bright, airy. Cream, sand, and soft-neutral backdrops, or simple sunlit interiors and outdoor lifestyle.
- **Wardrobe:** always the brand's own product. Denim, knits, tees, sweatshirts, tailored neutrals.
- **Attitude:** intentional but unfussy. Confident without performing.

The two muses then diverge on a single clear axis: **warm and relaxed** vs **cool and refined**. That axis covers the brand's two registers, the everyday core and the elevated edit, without fracturing the look.

## 6. The Consistency System (the part that actually matters)

A muse only works if the *same person* shows up across hundreds of images. Re-prompting a text description will never do this. It produces a new face every time. The mechanism is reference-locking:

1. **Generate a seed set.** Produce many images from each muse's identity block (Section 7). Judge purely on the face.
2. **Lock one hero face per muse.** Pick the single best, most on-brand frame. From this moment it is canonical. Do not silently replace it later.
3. **Build a reference pack.** From the locked hero, generate or shoot 3–5 clean angles of the same face: front, three-quarter left, three-quarter right, slight profile, neutral expression, plain background. This pack is the input for everything that follows.
4. **Generate from the reference, not from scratch.** Use a reference-capable model and feed it the locked reference plus a new prompt for the new outfit, pose, or scene. The identity holds; only the context changes.
5. **Version everything.** Date and label each reference pack (`GOLDEN_ref_v1_2026-06`). If you ever update a muse, treat it as a new version, communicated deliberately, not a quiet drift.

**Tool reality, stated honestly:**

- **Canva** (what we used so far) does *not* hold a character identity between generations. Use it for layout, social composition, and quick look-tests once you already have a locked muse. Do not use it to create the canonical face.
- **Your local `/ai-image-creator` skill** is the right tool for the locked, repeatable muse, because it supports reference-driven generation (the `-r` reference flag) with stronger photographic models (FLUX.2, SeedDream, Gemini). That is where the canonical faces and all production imagery should come from.
- I could not run that skill in this chat (its scripts are not mounted here and network is disabled), so the commands in Section 7 are written for you to run in your own Claude Code / Cowork setup. Confirm exact flag syntax against your installed skill's help before relying on it; I have not been able to execute it to verify.

## 7. Generation assets

### 7a. Identity blocks (reusable prompt text)

Paste the relevant block into any image tool, then add the outfit, pose, and scene for that specific shot. Keep the identity block *fixed* so the description never drifts.

**GOLDEN identity block:**

> A woman in her mid-twenties, slim and natural build, soft oval face, warm open expression. Golden honey blonde hair, collarbone-to-mid-back length, soft loose natural waves, centre part. Warm light sun-kissed skin with a light natural scatter of freckles across the nose and cheeks. Warm hazel-green almond eyes. Fresh, minimal "clean girl" makeup. Photographed in warm, bright, soft natural light.

**IVORY identity block:**

> A woman in her late twenties, slim with long elegant lines, refined oval face with defined cheekbones, calm composed expression. Cool icy-vanilla blonde hair, long to mid-back, sleek and smooth. Cool fair porcelain skin, clear and even, no freckles. Cool grey-blue almond eyes, direct gaze. Fresh, minimal "clean girl" makeup. Photographed in clean, bright, soft light.

### 7b. Canva (for look-tests and social layouts now)

Use the identity block plus a shot brief, and always include: *full-bleed photographic image, realistic, no text, no graphics, no logos, vertical portrait*. Remember Canva will give a new face each time, so treat these as mood, not canon.

### 7c. Local skill (for canonical faces and production)

Indicative structure only. Verify flags against your skill before use:

```
# Step 1 — generate seed set for the locked face (text-to-image, no reference yet)
generate-image -p "<GOLDEN identity block>, head-and-shoulders, neutral cream studio backdrop" --model flux2 -n 8

# Step 2 — once a hero face is chosen and saved as golden_ref.png,
#          generate production shots FROM that reference
generate-image -r golden_ref.png -p "<scene: e.g. wearing vintage-wash denim and a cream mockneck, sitting on a sunlit kitchen counter, holding an iced matcha>" --model flux2
```

Repeat for IVORY with `seedream` or `flux2` and the IVORY block. Lock the reference first; everything downstream points at it.

## 8. Guardrails: do and don't

**Do**

- Keep both muses in real product, in warm/bright/airy light.
- Keep makeup minimal and skin natural.
- Hold each muse to her locked reference for every new image.
- Let GOLDEN carry candid lifestyle and IVORY carry hero/editorial.

**Don't**

- Mix the two tonal palettes on one muse (no warm freckled IVORY, no icy GOLDEN).
- Over-style or over-pose. The brand reads unfussy; heavy glam breaks it.
- Re-generate a face from text when you have a reference to hand. That is how drift starts.
- Copy Parke's actual campaign imagery, crops, or trade dress (see below).

## 9. Risk & Compliance Flags

These sit outside design and I am not giving legal advice. Each is worth qualified professional input before you scale.

**a. Disclosure of AI-generated models.** Using synthetic people as brand "models" in advertising is a developing regulatory area. UK (ASA / CAP Code) and US (FTC) both scrutinise materially misleading representations, and platforms including TikTok Shop maintain their own AI-content and disclosure policies that change often. Before you run AI muses at scale on TikTok Shop, confirm current disclosure requirements with a marketing/advertising-law adviser and check TikTok Shop's live policy. I have flagged this rather than asserted a specific rule because the position is genuinely in flux and I would not want to give you a confident answer that has since moved.

**b. IP and brand-clone risk.** Take Parke as directional inspiration only. Replicating its specific imagery, logos, garment trade dress, or overall "look and feel" closely enough to confuse a customer invites passing-off / unfair-competition exposure. Keep your muses, names, and brand world distinct.

**c. Reputational adjacency.** Parke is currently the subject of public authenticity allegations (claims it resells factory-made garments at a markup, and questions over whether its "sold out" scarcity is engineered). Building a brand that reads as an obvious Parke clone borrows that scrutiny. A differentiated identity is the safer long-term play, not just the more original one.

**d. Likeness.** Some image models can reproduce recognisable real people. Check each locked face does not resemble an identifiable individual, to avoid personality / publicity-rights issues.

## 10. Open decisions for you

The brief gave me "blonde, creamy, slim, Parke vibe." I have made deliberate choices to fill the gaps. Push back on any of these:

1. **The two are both blonde and white-coded, per your brief.** Worth a conscious decision rather than a default: an all-blonde muse pair narrows who sees themselves in the brand, and Parke's own draw is partly that customers picture *themselves*. A deliberate point of range (even keeping both blonde but varying features) could widen the market. Your call, but I would not let it pass unexamined.
2. **Two muses, warm vs cool.** An alternative is one hero muse plus a rotating supporting cast, which some DTC brands find easier to keep consistent. Two is workable; I have built for two.
3. **Names.** "GOLDEN" and "IVORY" are internal codenames chosen to avoid biasing AI models toward a real person. Rename for your own use; keep public-facing names distinct from real public figures.
4. **The claw-clip nod on IVORY.** It ties to Parke's signature, which is good for resonance and slightly risky for distinctiveness. Keep, soften, or drop.
